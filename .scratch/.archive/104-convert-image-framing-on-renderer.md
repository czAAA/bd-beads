# 104: Convert image framing on the renderer

**What to build:** The Convert image framing preview is drawn by the Pattern renderer instead of one DOM element per bead, so dragging the picture under the frame is smooth at the sizes that are slow today (measured before this work: about 50 ms per drag step at 60×90 and 60 ms at 100×100, against a 16 ms budget). The frame outline and its dimming of everything outside stay as they are; only the beads move to a Drawing surface. The old DOM preview is replaced outright (it needs no reference switch: ticket 103's screenshots are the reference), and its tests move with it.

**Approximate colors while dragging.** Reducing the picture to the chosen number of Image colors costs more than the whole frame budget on its own, so while the picture is being dragged the preview keeps the colors it had when the drag started and maps each bead to the nearest of them; when the drag ends (or pauses) it recomputes exactly. At rest the preview, and so the Pattern that Create makes, is exactly what it is today: "what is inside the frame is exactly the Pattern that will be created" (ADR 0010) still holds whenever the picture is not moving. Zooming the picture and changing the color count recompute exactly, as now.

## How it came out

- **What changed.** `ConvertImageFrame.vue` draws its beads with the Pattern renderer onto one canvas (`renderPattern`, block of beads shaped like a Pattern, no Row progress, upright), fitted to the panel and sharp on high-density screens. The frame outline and its dimming are the same element as before, over the canvas. The per-bead elements, their styles and the row elements are gone; the tests that found them (`ConvertImageFrame.test.ts`, `App.convertImage.test.ts`) now assert on the beads handed to the renderer's bead drawer and on the lattice size the canvas states (`data-columns`/`data-rows`), with a fake canvas installed for jsdom (`src/testUtils/fakeCanvas.ts`). Every behaviour they covered is still covered (bead rendering per Technique incl. peyote's stagger and radius, frame over exactly the Pattern's cells and offset past overhang, reflow on zoom, Create emitting what is on screen, drag, colors).
- **Colors while dragging.** While the picture is being dragged, every bead (the frame's included) is the nearest of the Image colors the conversion had when the drag began (`approximatePreviewColors`, `src/domain/framingPreview.ts`); when the drag ends, or the pointer pauses for 150 ms, the exact conversion is recomputed for the current pan and the beads and the "Image colors: N" count follow it. Zooming, changing the color count and Reset recompute exactly, as before. Create always reads the exact conversion. Tested: held colors while moving, exact on release, exact after a pause and held from then, no recompute while the pointer keeps moving, and that a dragged-to pan creates exactly what a fresh picture at rest at that pan creates.
- **At rest it is exactly what it was.** Sampling now works on packed integer colors a row at a time (`sampleLatticePacked`), which is what makes a lattice of thousands of beads cheap enough to redo on every move; a test shows it takes bit-for-bit the same colors as sampling cell by cell with `cellCenter`/`sourcePixelAt`/`pixelColorAt`, in all three Techniques at several zooms and pans, and `exactPreviewColors` is tested equal to the previous chain (`convertSampledFrame` + `previewColorOutsideFrame`) for the frame and the surround. `convertPackedFrame` is tested equal to `convertSampledFrame`. So the Image colors count shown and the Pattern created are unchanged for the same picture, frame, zoom, pan and color count.
- **Brick stitch's preview was clipped and its frame drifted, and no longer is.** The old DOM preview gave each brick row a 1px seam that the box's size and the frame outline's position (both from `rowHeightPx`, 20px) left out, so the bottom rows were cut off and the frame sat `frameRow` px above the beads it was meant to outline. The box, the frame and the drag ratio now come from the renderer's own geometry (21px), so the whole lattice shows and the frame lands on bead rows (tested). The three brick framing references from ticket 103 were regenerated for it; loom and peyote references are unchanged.
- **Drawing cost.** Made the renderer's default bead cheap, because the drag redraws thousands of beads per move: a square bead is two `fillRect`s, and rounded or faded beads are kept as small bitmaps (made at the size the bead is on the screen's pixels, blitted without smoothing) and blitted. Measured on the way: path fills per bead were ~7 µs each and dominated the frame (9–10 fps for peyote); batching a row into one path was *slower* (`closePath` costs by subpath count, and big paths rasterize slowly); bitmaps roughly doubled peyote. The renderer's browser check against the DOM references still passes.
- **The framing visual check** compares the canvas against ticket 103's references with a 0.8% pixel budget (the edge anti-aliasing of beads at the preview's fractional fit scale; one bead is about 0.6% of the picture), instead of the DOM's near-exact one; what each bead is drawn in is pinned by the component's own tests.

### Performance (`npm run perf`, framing drag, 60×90 and 100×100 frames; before this ticket: loom 18–21 fps at 4× and 15 at 6×)

| Technique | 4× slowdown | 6× slowdown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| loom | 60×90: **43 fps** (18 ms) · 100×100: **48 fps** (16 ms) | 32 fps (27 ms) · 33 fps (25 ms) |
| brick stitch | 60×90: **42 fps** (19 ms) · 100×100: **50 fps** (15 ms) | 30 fps (29 ms) · 34 fps (24 ms) |
| peyote | 60×90: 23 fps (40 ms) · 100×100: 21 fps (43 ms) | 15 fps (62 ms) · 13 fps (75 ms) |

Loom and brick stitch hold the floor (30 fps at 4×) with room to spare, and even at 6×. **Peyote does not: 21–23 fps at 4×, about 2.3× better than before but under the floor.** Its rounded beads are bitmap blits, ~1.5–3 µs each in this software-rendered headless browser (a GPU-backed canvas on a real tablet will be cheaper, but that is unmeasured), and the lattice is up to 12,000 beads. Left for later, not hidden: redraw only the beads whose color changed (and the neighbors that nest over them), and blit at whole device pixels. The same cost will matter for scrolling a big peyote Pattern in ticket 105, so it is better solved there, in the renderer, than here.

### The slow-framing hint (a requirement this write-up left out)
The New Pattern form's hint that framing "may feel slow" (from `SLOW_FRAMING_CELLS`, 12,000 cells per Technique) and `PREVIEW_MAX_CELLS` (12,000, the most beads the surround of the frame may add) were reviewed and **kept as they are**: the drawing is far cheaper than the elements were, but the numbers above show framing is still the slowest thing in the app (a 60×90 peyote frame is under the floor at 4×, and 250×250 is 6–9 fps in every Technique), so the hint is still true at the size it fires and the cap still bounds the cost of the surround. They should move only with a faster framing drag (ticket 122).
