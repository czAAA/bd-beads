# A Pattern has no size limit: the device is the limit

Supersedes the total-cell cap in [ADR 0017](0017-grid-is-the-size-mm-is-an-estimate.md). Follows from [ADR 0018](0018-pattern-drawn-by-one-renderer-not-a-dom-cell-per-bead.md), whose consequences said the cap would go once the editor drew on the renderer.

ADR 0017 capped a Pattern at 10,000 cells (columns × rows) when it was created, converted from a picture or grown by Resize, because every cell was a DOM element and a paint or hover step cost about 3.4µs per cell: 34ms at 100 × 100 and 300ms at 250 × 250. That cost is gone. The Pattern is drawn by one renderer onto a surface as big as the screen, an edit redraws the few rows it touched, and what a paint or hover step costs no longer depends on how big the Pattern is. So the cap is removed everywhere it applied: the New Pattern form (in beads, mm or cm), Convert image, and Resize, along with the refusal messages and their translations. A Pattern of 70 × 250 (a bracelet: 17,500 cells) or 250 × 250 (62,500) is now created, edited, saved, reloaded, exported as a Pattern file and undone like any other, and Convert image can frame a picture at either size. Patterns already over the old cap (imported ones, say) were never blocked and are unchanged.

**What limits a Pattern instead is the device**, and the tests behind this decision looked at each in turn:

- **Speed.** On a production build with the CPU slowed 4× (a midrange Android tablet or a Core i3 laptop), hover, paint, Selection drag and paste hover hold 60 fps at 60 × 90, 70 × 250 and 250 × 250, scrolling holds 54 fps at 70 × 250, and opening a Pattern paints in about 140 ms at 70 × 250 and about 260 ms at 250 × 250 (from 950 ms and 3 s). Ticket 122 brought the last numbers above the floor: scrolling a 250 × 250 Pattern holds 60 fps (it is held whole rather than as a window, so scrolling never draws), and Convert image's framing drag holds 38–60 fps in every Technique at 60 × 90, 100 × 100, 70 × 250 and 250 × 250, by drawing a coarse look (one flat color per bead, no rims) for as long as the picture moves in a block too big to draw bead by bead; the beads are back when it is at rest, and the New Pattern form's hint (framing "may feel slow" from 12,000 cells) still stands for the cost of the exact colors. A manual pass on an iPad Air 13″ (60 fps at 250 × 250 was the goal) has not been made and is still to do.
- **Storage.** A Pattern is stored compactly (ADR 0009). Measured: a 250 × 250 Pattern is 0.4 KB empty, 31 KB painted in blocks of color and 136 KB as noise (twelve colors at random, the worst case); a 70 × 250 one is 0.3, 9 and 38 KB. A browser's localStorage holds about 5 MB, so a library of the noisiest 250 × 250 Patterns fills it at about 35. When a save does not fit, ADR 0012 applies unchanged: the top bar says the change is not saved and it stays on screen (checked at 70 × 250 and 250 × 250 with a full device).
- **Memory.** A Pattern is one object per cell, about 60,000 of them at 250 × 250, and Undo keeps whole-grid snapshots. An edit now shares the rows it does not touch, so a paint or Mirror stroke adds only the rows it changed to the history; a Fill or Paste of a big area still adds a full grid. Ticket 112 measured it: 300 one-bead strokes add about 3 MB to the page's heap and 40 whole-Pattern Fills of a 250 × 250 Pattern about 128 MB (3.2 MB each). That was judged acceptable and ticket 112 closed without a change; interning cells (one shared object per color) would cut a Fill's cost to a sixth if it ever matters.

**Considered options**: keeping a cap and raising it (to 62,500, say) — rejected: there is no measured cliff to put it at, every size tested up to 250 × 250 works, and a number chosen without one would be as arbitrary as the one it replaced and would need moving again; keeping a warning instead of a refusal — rejected for now, since nothing measured says a person would be hurt by a big Pattern that a message would prevent (the framing hint, which does have a measured reason, stays); refusing only when the device's storage is nearly full — rejected, ADR 0012's message after a failed save already says exactly that, when it is true, and does not guess.

**Consequences.**
- QR sharing (ADR 0015) stays capacity-capped: a Pattern too large for one code is turned away by the QR control and shared as a Pattern file, as before. Big Patterns will nearly all take that path.
- If a real device runs out of memory or storage with big Patterns in practice, the way to a limit is a measured one, recorded in a new ADR; this one says only that no limit is set by size alone.
