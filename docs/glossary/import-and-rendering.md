# Glossary: Import and rendering

Convert image, the Project renderer and the Bead pointer. Part of the glossary split out of `CONTEXT.md` (ticket 380); the index is [README.md](README.md).

**Convert image**: Sized by a **Width** and a **Height** in beads over the part of the picture to weave (tickets 0747, 0757): the whole picture, or **A part**, drawn on the preview like a Selection (drag to draw it, drag outside to draw a new one, drag inside to move it, pull its handles to adjust it). Choosing the part only chooses *what* is woven: the Width and Height stay as they were, dropping only when the part has fewer pixels than they allow. A **chain** between the two sliders keeps them linked by default: moving either sets the other through the picture's shape and the Bead's aspect, so the picture is never distorted by accident; unlinked, each is set on its own and the picture stretches on purpose. Each runs from 8 beads to one bead per picture pixel (at most 300, the Large Schema line; a side under 75 px may go up to 4× its pixels), and the Width opens at its maximum. On a **mark**, where one bead is a whole n × n pixels (or one pixel a whole k × k beads), the Schema is the pixel grid exactly, as pixel art needs; between marks the other side follows the picture's physical shape through the Bead, so a non-square Bead makes the piece a little wider or taller than the picture on a mark, which the result line's cm shows. Unlinked, each slider keeps its own marks. Colors are one count, from 1 to every color the part has, at most 256.
Creating a Project from a picture instead of an empty grid, on its own **App page** (the **Convert image page**, ADR 0086, spec 0645): it fills the canvas box in place of the Canvas, with the editor's side panels hidden and the strip keeping Menu and a Close (×). A live preview of the picture rendered as beads, with the Frame over it, sits beside one of five steps at a time, Picture, Bead & Technique, Size, Colors and Review (above it under 1024 px or in portrait); the Progress bar holds the steps as named dots with Back and Next, and Next is Create Project on Review. Once a picture is set, Close, Escape and every leaving Menu action ask "Leave the converter?" (Leave, or Back to converter, the default); with no picture it closes at once. Either way the person lands on the Project that was open, its Undo and Redo untouched, and converting is no Undo step. The Menu has a Convert image row under New Project. It holds its own copy of the New Project form's Bead and Technique, and the device remembers none of its choices. It takes photos as well as pixel art and flat artwork: what falls inside the Frame becomes the Project's grid bead by bead, each bead at its true centre taking the color most of its area has, unless a thin, strongly contrasting line crosses it, which then wins, so a photo is not speckled, outlines and strokes stay, and pixel art on a mark is its pixels exactly (while a handle moves, the preview takes each bead's centre pixel; ticket 0762). Each of its colors is matched, measured in OKLab (ADR 0010, ticket 0634), to the nearest color of the chosen source: the Bead's palette (from the Bead's Color catalog), the default Palette, or Exact (the picture's own), which is the default (ticket 0758). Treat as empty is a switch in the Colors step, and a found color is changed through the app's own colour popover body. Each color found can be kept exact, remapped or set to empty; the new Project's Palette is its built-in colors plus every color its beads use, as ordinary swatches. Review shows the app's own Beads needed for that Project, closed with its total on the row (ticket 0759), never a list of its own. A way of creating a Project only, never a command that converts into one already open. Built in ticket 0649, made a page in ticket 0756.
_Avoid_: import image, trace, pixelate, image import

**Project renderer**:
The one thing that draws a Project's cells — for the editor, the Convert image preview and the exports — so a bead looks the same wherever it appears. Draws whatever part of the Project is in view, at the current zoom and rotation, in the Technique's geometry. One renderer over two spaces: the canvas of the editor, where every position is real and the Frame sits where it is, and the Frame alone of the exports, the Convert image preview and the Homepage, where the Frame's first bead is position (0, 0). The overlay layer is drawn in the same space.
_Avoid_: grid component, exporter, preview renderer

**Bead rim** (ticket 0460):
A faint line just inside every bead's edge (0.75px), so where one bead ends and the next starts shows even when both are the same color. Every theme has one: a dark tint in light and high contrast, a white tint in dark. The same on every bead and in every Technique, 0.75px in the Project's own px so it shrinks with zoom with the bead; exports and printed pages draw their own (light) rim, unchanged. It sits under every overlay (the Frame, the Selection, the Bead pointer, the Row progress marks).
_Avoid_: border, outline (the hover outline is another thing), grid line, gap

**Drawing surface**:
What the Project renderer draws on inside the canvas panel: a base layer holding the cells, and an overlay layer holding everything that comes and goes with the pointer — hover preview, the bead pointer, Selection, paste preview and the Row progress marker. It is not the "canvas" of the App shell layout, which is the panel that holds it.
_Avoid_: canvas (that is the panel), bitmap, canvas element

**Bead pointer** (ticket 353):
What the pointer is over the beads of an open Project, for a mouse: a small bead-shaped marker, 90% of a bead as drawn less 1px (so it follows zoom), centred on the pointer rather than snapped to the bead under it (a hovering pen's stands 18px above its tip and to the side away from the writing hand, set by the Menu's Hand row, Right until Left is chosen and kept on the device, so the hand does not cover it), rounded in peyote and square otherwise, and looking like the hover preview (the chosen color at 60%, else the 2px dark outline; the preview fills only its bead, in the bead's own shape, and on peyote leaves out the part the next row's beads lie over). The OS pointer is hidden there unless a Tool pointer takes its place (every tool has one, so over the canvas the marker is not drawn, for a mouse or a pen); a finger shows none, and a hovering pen has the Tool pointer the app draws instead (ticket 546). The other pointers stay: not-allowed over the Frame's margin, the Hand tool's Tool pointer, grab for Space, and the Set Frame cursor.
_Avoid_: crosshair

**Tool pointer** (ticket 0542):
The pointer over the canvas that shows which tool is in use: a small drawing of the tool (a pencil, an eraser, a hand, the arrow, a bucket, a piece of beads), drawn lightly so it stays out of the painting, with a bead in the current colour on Paint and Fill. Hand's closes while it pans. The files are the design system's `assets/Cursors/`, since the browser calls them CSS cursors. A mouse or trackpad gets it as the CSS cursor; a hovering Apple Pencil gets none from Safari, so the app draws the same file itself at the Pencil's position, hot spot on the tip (ticket 546). That drawing is for hover only (hidden while the Pencil touches the screen, when it lifts away or is cancelled, and when it leaves the board), only for a pointer that draws with the tool (in Mouse mode the Pencil moves the canvas, so it shows the Hand's open hand), and includes the eyedropper's pointer (ticket 0403). Over the Frame's margin and with the Set Frame cursor the Pencil shows nothing, as the hover preview already refuses there. Only a hover-capable iPad (M2 iPad Pro or later, with Pencil 2 or Pencil Pro) sends pen hover; a finger or another iPad shows nothing before contact.
_Avoid_: tool cursor, tool icon (that is the Toolbox button's glyph)

**Letters** (ticket 0573; export review 2026-10-27): the Frame panel toggle, off by default and saved with the Project, that shows each bead's Swatch letter on the Frame's beads in the editor, upright on screen however the Canvas, the Frame or the bead is turned (ticket 0728) and adds the Word chart to the PDF. It never changes the beads' look in an export: the PDF's chart pages always carry the letters (a color-only chart cannot be read in black and white print) and the PNG never does (it is the picture, not the pattern).
_Avoid_: labels, symbols mode

**Word chart** (ticket 0574): the written pattern a weaver follows beside the picture, made when Letters is on: a legend (letter, color name, bead count), then one line per row, such as `Row 3 (R): 2C, 1A, 3*` (a count and a Swatch letter, `*` for empty cells), in the order of Row progress (Row 1 is the first row the Progress bar counts, said in the header). Pages after the Specification in the PDF only, never a PNG; a row never splits across pages, each page's title names the rows it holds, and rows short enough run in two columns. One line per row, never "same as row n": the weaver ticks rows one at a time. Its header says where Row 1 is on the picture (rotation included) and what a run is; an (L) row is read left to right on the picture, an (R) row right to left. For peyote the first line is `Rows 1&2` (the first pass, as the Pass term defines) and every later line is one Pass, with a header saying Even count or Odd count; peyote and brick stitch lines carry an alternating (L) or (R), loom lines read left to right with no marker. Its words are translated; its letters and numbers are not.
_Avoid_: instructions page, bead list, legend (that is only its first part)

**Specification** (ticket 0581): the PDF page after the chart pages that a weaver takes to the shop: the full Beads needed table (letter, swatch, color, brand code, beads, grams, **to buy**), a Total row, the grams note, the Schema's facts and a map of the parts with their pages. **To buy** is tubes of 5 g from the color's grams with about 10% spare, rounded up ("2 × 5 g"), so a color under a gram is one tube; the Total adds the rows; an unknown Bead has no grams and no tubes. The Word chart follows it.
_Avoid_: shopping list, summary page

**Export quality**:
The on-device choice, below the export buttons in the Frame panel, of how sharp the PNG is: Low (saves memory), Medium (the default) or High, its pages drawn at 150, 300 or 450 dots per inch (ticket 0583). The PDF has no quality: its chart pages are drawn as lines and shapes, sharp at any zoom, and its other pages are pictures at 300 dpi (export review 2026-10-27). A device that cannot hold a 450 dpi page (found by trying to allocate one) draws High at 300 dpi and says so under the slider. Pictures are lossless, not JPEG (ADR 0043).

**File size estimate**: the size each export button and Export quality stop shows before exporting: the bytes of every file the button gives, rounded **up** to 0.1 MB, with the page or file count ("PDF ≈ 3.2 MB · 9 pages"). It is an upper bound: the sum of its pages, each by its kind and content (Cover, list page, vector chart page, Specification, Word chart page), so the real file is never larger. A Cover with many beads, or a photograph's noise, can come out up to about half the figure.
_Avoid_: resolution, picture size (confusable with the Frame's size)

**Cover page** (ticket 0583): the one A4 portrait page that opens both the PDF and the PNG export, drawn by the same code. Inside a 10 mm margin on every side it holds, top to bottom: the Frame's name as the title, else the Project's (left out when both are empty; ticket 0720), "by {Maker's name}" under it (left out when empty), one tech line (technique, Bead, grid, Estimated size), the framed Schema, Beads needed (two columns, the summary line, Total, the spares note) and the footer (wordmark, date and time). A small Schema's beads are capped at about 6 mm and the picture is centred in the room left, with Beads needed moved up under it at an even gap; a big Schema is scaled down to fit; a wide flat one fits the page width. Beads needed that does not fit continues on **list pages** after it, in the PDF as pages and in the PNG as further files, numbered `_2`, `_3` after the export's file name. The PNG no longer has a full-detail tall picture: the PDF's chart pages carry the detail. In every export an empty position is no bead: bare board on the Cover and in the PNG, plain paper with no letter on a chart page, so a White bead (drawn as a bead, with its letter) never reads as empty.
_Avoid_: story, header block (the old PNG's name for the text under the chart)
