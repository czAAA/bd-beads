# Glossary: Import and rendering

Convert image, the Project renderer and the Bead pointer. Part of the glossary split out of `CONTEXT.md` (ticket 380); the index is [README.md](README.md).

**Convert image**: Sized by a **Width** and a **Height** in beads over the part of the picture to weave (tickets 0747, 0757): the **Whole picture**, or **A part of the picture**, a crop drawn on the preview with **Select** like a Selection (drag to draw it, drag outside to draw a new one, drag inside to move it, pull its handles to adjust it). Choosing the crop only chooses *what* is woven: the Width and Height stay as they were, dropping only when the crop has fewer pixels than they allow. **Keep the picture's shape** (ticket 0780; it replaced the chain of 0757) is a switch between the two sliders, on by default: moving either sets the other through the picture's shape and the Bead's aspect, so the picture is never distorted by accident; off, each is set on its own and the picture stretches on purpose. Each runs from 8 beads to one bead per picture pixel (at most 300, the Large Schema line; a side under 75 px may go up to 4× its pixels), and the Width opens at its maximum. On a **mark**, where one bead is a whole n × n pixels (or one pixel a whole k × k beads), the Schema is the pixel grid exactly, as pixel art needs; between marks the other side follows the picture's physical shape through the Bead, so a non-square Bead makes the piece a little wider or taller than the picture on a mark, which the result line's cm shows. With the shape not kept, each slider keeps its own marks. Colors are one count, from 1 to every color the part has, at most 256.
Creating a Project from a picture instead of an empty grid, on its own **App page** (the **Convert image page**, ADR 0086, spec 0645): it fills the canvas box in place of the Canvas, with the editor's side panels hidden and the strip keeping Menu and a Close (×). A live preview of the picture rendered as beads, with the Frame over it, sits beside one scrolling pane of five steps, Picture & bead, Background, Size, Colors and Review, each header pinned while its step is in view (above it under 1024 px or in portrait; ticket 0780). The Progress bar holds the steps as named dots with ← Back and Next →, its fill following the pane's scroll; a dot, Back or Next scrolls to its step, and Next is Create Project on Review. A band of facts above the preview gives the grid size, its measure and the colors; a floating bar under it holds Turn 90°, Flip, the Select, **Make empty** and **Background** tools, **Compare** (the original picture while held) and zoom. **Background** (ticket 0780; it replaced Cut out) takes away what is round the subject in two stages: Remove background finds it from the picture's edges inward (the border's color families, less those too small or more in the picture's middle than at its edge, and every bead within a Tolerance of one, in OKLab; ticket 0787) and hatches it faded on the preview (Find), and Remove what was found makes those beads empty places, so the colors are counted from what is left. The **Background tool** on the floating bar fixes what Find got wrong: a tap or a drag over found beads keeps the patch under it, over kept beads makes it background, each one a step that Undo, Redo and Start over in the Background step take back or put back; the steps are kept in picture pixels and replayed over a new Tolerance, Width, Height or crop, and dropped by a turn, a flip or a new picture. **Keeping a part of the beads**: with Whole picture, a drag with Select on the beads keeps only those, drawn as a Selection, with nothing converted again; Keep all, or a new Width or Height, clears it, and the Size result, Beads needed and the created Project hold only the kept beads. Once a picture is set, Close, Escape and every leaving Menu action ask "Leave the converter?" (Leave, or Back to converter, the default); with no picture it closes at once. Either way the person lands on the Project that was open, its Undo and Redo untouched, and converting is no Undo step. The Menu has a Convert image row under New Project. It holds its own copy of the New Project form's Bead and Technique; the device remembers the last conversion's Bead, Technique with its Drop and source of the colors, and the page opens on them unless the form's Bead or Technique was changed in the same visit, the first time on Miyuki Delica 11/0 in peyote (ticket 0893). Width, the number of colors and the crop are never remembered. What falls inside the Frame becomes the Project's grid bead by bead, each bead taking the color most of its area has round its true centre, unless a thin, strongly contrasting line crosses it, which then wins, so a photo is less speckled, outlines and strokes stay, and pixel art is its pixels exactly (while a handle moves, the preview takes each bead's centre pixel; ticket 0888). Each of its colors is matched, measured in OKLab (ADR 0010, ticket 0634), to the nearest color of the chosen source: the Bead's palette (from the Bead's Color catalog), the default Palette, or Exact (the picture's own), which is the default (ticket 0758). **Make empty** (ticket 0780; it replaced Treat as empty's switch) is the preview's eyedropper: a tap leaves that found color out everywhere and a tap on its empty place brings it back, in step with Color by color's Empty buttons; a found color is changed through the app's own colour popover body. Each color found can be kept exact, remapped or set to empty; the new Project's Palette is its built-in colors plus every color its beads use, as ordinary swatches. Review shows the app's own Beads needed for that Project, closed with its total on the row (ticket 0759), never a list of its own. A way of creating a Project only, never a command that converts into one already open. Built in ticket 0649, made a page in ticket 0756, redesigned for desktop in ticket 0780.
_Avoid_: import image, trace, pixelate, image import; Treat as empty, Cut out, the chain (the old names of Make empty, Background and Keep the picture's shape)

**Project renderer**:
The one thing that draws a Project's cells — for the editor, the Convert image preview and the exports — so a bead looks the same wherever it appears. Draws whatever part of the Project is in view, at the current zoom and rotation, in the Technique's geometry. One renderer over two spaces: the canvas of the editor, where every position is real and the Frame sits where it is, and the Frame alone of the exports, the Convert image preview and the Homepage, where the Frame's first bead is position (0, 0). The overlay layer is drawn in the same space.
_Avoid_: grid component, exporter, preview renderer

**Bead rim** (ticket 0460):
A faint line just inside every bead's edge (0.75px), so where one bead ends and the next starts shows even when both are the same color. Every theme has one: a dark tint in light and high contrast, a white tint in dark. The same on every bead and in every Technique, 0.75px in the Project's own px so it shrinks with zoom with the bead; exports and printed pages draw their own (light) rim, unchanged. It sits under every overlay (the Frame, the Selection, the Bead pointer, the Row progress marks).
_Avoid_: border, outline (the hover outline is another thing), grid line, gap

**Drawing surface**:
What the Project renderer draws on inside the canvas panel: a base layer holding the cells, and an overlay layer holding everything that comes and goes with the pointer — hover preview, the bead pointer, Selection, paste preview and the Row progress marker. It is not the "canvas" of the App shell layout, which is the panel that holds it.
_Avoid_: canvas (that is the panel), bitmap, canvas element

**Bead pointer** (ticket 353):
What the pointer is over the beads of an open Project, for a mouse: a small bead-shaped marker, 90% of a bead as drawn less 1px (so it follows zoom), centred on the pointer rather than snapped to the bead under it (a hovering pen's stands 18px above its tip and to the side away from the writing hand, set by the Menu's Hand row, Right until Left is chosen and kept on the device, so the hand does not cover it), rounded in peyote and square otherwise, and looking like the hover preview (the chosen color at 60%, else the 2px dark outline; the preview fills only its bead, in the bead's own shape, and on peyote leaves out the part the next row's beads lie over). The OS pointer is hidden there unless a Tool pointer takes its place (every tool has one, so over the canvas the marker is not drawn, for a mouse or a pen); a finger shows none, and a hovering pen has the Tool pointer the app draws instead (ticket 546). The other pointers stay: not-allowed over the Frame's margin, the Hand tool's Tool pointer, grab for Space, and the Set Frame cursor.
_Avoid_: crosshair

**Tool pointer** (ticket 0542):
The pointer over the canvas that shows which tool is in use: a small drawing of the tool (a pencil, an eraser, a hand, the arrow, a bucket, a piece of beads), drawn lightly so it stays out of the painting, with a bead in the current colour on Paint and Fill. Hand's closes while it pans. The files are the design system's `assets/Cursors/`, since the browser calls them CSS cursors. A mouse or trackpad gets it as the CSS cursor; a hovering Apple Pencil gets none from Safari, so the app draws the same file itself at the Pencil's position, hot spot on the tip (ticket 546). That drawing is for hover only (hidden while the Pencil touches the screen, when it lifts away or is cancelled, and when it leaves the board), only for a pointer that draws with the tool (in Mouse mode the Pencil moves the canvas, so it shows the Hand's open hand), and includes the eyedropper's pointer (ticket 0403). Over the Frame's margin and with the Set Frame cursor the Pencil shows nothing, as the hover preview already refuses there. Only a hover-capable iPad (M2 iPad Pro or later, with Pencil 2 or Pencil Pro) sends pen hover; a finger or another iPad shows nothing before contact.
_Avoid_: tool cursor, tool icon (that is the Toolbox button's glyph)

**Letters** (ticket 0573; export review 2026-10-27): the Frame panel toggle, off by default and saved with the Project, that shows each bead's Swatch letter on the Frame's beads in the editor, upright on screen however the Canvas, the Frame or the bead is turned (ticket 0728). It adds or removes no PDF page: the Word chart is Row by row's (ticket 0916). It never changes the beads' look in an export: every chart, in the PDF and in the PNG (page 1 as a picture, ticket 0915), always carries the letters (a color-only chart cannot be read in black and white print), and the overview of a piece cut into parts never does.
_Avoid_: labels, symbols mode

**Word chart** (ticket 0574): the written pattern a weaver follows beside the picture, made when **Row by row** is on in the Export panel (ticket 0916; Letters until then): a legend (letter, color name, bead count), then one line per row, such as `Row 3 (R): 2C, 1A, 3*` (a count and a Swatch letter, `*` for empty cells), in the order of Row progress (Row 1 is the first row the Progress bar counts, said in the header). Pages after the chart in the PDF only, never a PNG; a row never splits across pages, each page's title names the rows it holds, and rows short enough run in two columns. One line per row, never "same as row n": the weaver ticks rows one at a time. Its header says where Row 1 is on the picture (rotation included) and what a run is; an (L) row is read left to right on the picture, an (R) row right to left. For peyote the first line is `Rows 1&2` (the first pass, as the Pass term defines) and every later line is one Pass, with a header saying Even count or Odd count; peyote and brick stitch lines carry an alternating (L) or (R), loom lines read left to right with no marker. A round of a part worked in rounds (Triangle, Warped Square, a Kaleidocycle's triangle, a Star's square) is written side by side from its corner pair (ticket 0873): each side its pair in brackets, `(2A)`, or `(1A 1B)` for a pair of two colors in the order they are picked up, then its singles' runs, the sides apart at a `|`, as `Round 3: (2A), 1A | (2A), 1A | (2A), 1A | (2A), 1A`; a pair's two colors and a side's `|` never wrap away from it, and the header says what a pair and a `|` are. Round 1, the ring, is a plain run (`4A`). A Kite's row (ticket 0874) carries its (L) or (R), as it runs back and forth, and its middle pair, the spine pair, in brackets as a corner pair is: `Row 3 (L): 1A, (2B), 1A`. Its words are translated; its letters and numbers are not.
_Avoid_: instructions page, bead list, legend (that is only its first part)

**Large beads** and **Row by row** (ticket 0916): the Printable chart's two switches in the Export panel (and the Frame panel's export section), off until switched on and remembered on the device, not with the Project. Large beads keeps every bead of the PDF's charts at 3 mm or more (`print-bead-large`), at the cost of more pages; Row by row adds the Word chart's pages. The Printable chart row counts the PDF's pages with them before the export.

**Page 1** (ticket 0914): the PDF's first page, and for most pieces the whole PDF: the title block (the Frame's name, else the Project's; "by {Maker's name}"; one tech line), the whole piece, one **Beads needed** table and the footer with the page number ("1 / 1"). When the chart fits page 1 at a working bead (up to about 100 × 100, a bracelet folded into strips), page 1 is the chart itself, lettered and numbered as a chart page, and nothing follows but list pages and the Word chart. When it does not, page 1 is the overview, the whole piece without letters with the parts marked on it and their pages, and the chart pages follow. Beads needed is printed once: a letter swatch, color name, brand code, beads, grams and **to buy** for each color, in two columns reading down, then the Total (beads, grams, tubes) and the grams note. **To buy** is tubes of 5 g from the color's grams with about 10% spare, rounded up ("2 × 5 g"), so a color under a gram is one tube; the Total adds the rows; an unknown Bead has no grams, no tubes and no note. Colors that do not fit continue on **list pages** straight after page 1. It replaced the Cover page and the Specification page in the PDF; the Specification's facts are page 1's tech line, its table is Beads needed, its part map the overview.
_Avoid_: shopping list, summary page

**Export quality**:
The on-device choice, below the export buttons in the Frame panel, of how sharp the PNG is: Low (saves memory), Medium (the default) or High, its pages drawn at 150, 300 or 450 dots per inch (ticket 0583). The PDF has no quality: its chart pages are drawn as lines and shapes, sharp at any zoom, and its other pages are pictures at 300 dpi (export review 2026-10-27). A device that cannot hold a 450 dpi page (found by trying to allocate one) draws High at 300 dpi and says so under the slider. Pictures are lossless, not JPEG (ADR 0043).

**File size estimate**: the size each export button shows before exporting: the bytes of every file the button gives, rounded **up** to 0.1 MB, with the page or file count ("PDF ≈ 3.2 MB · 9 pages"). It is an upper bound: the sum of its pages, each by its kind and content (page 1, list page, vector chart page, Word chart page), so the real file is never larger. Page 1 with many beads, or a photograph's noise, can come out up to about half the figure.
_Avoid_: resolution, picture size (confusable with the Frame's size)

**PNG export** (tickets 0583, 0915): page 1 of the PDF as a picture, one A4 portrait page drawn by the same code at the Export quality's dpi: the lettered chart with Beads needed when the piece fits page 1, else the overview without the lines where the parts meet and their pills (the PNG has no pages to point at). Beads needed that does not fit continues on page 1's **list pages**, as further PNG files numbered `_2`, `_3` after the export's file name. It was the **Cover page** until ticket 0915: the whole Schema on the board, its beads capped at about 6 mm, with no Swatch letters, also the PDF's first page until Page 1 replaced it there (ticket 0914). In every export an empty position is no bead: bare board on the overview, plain paper with no letter on a chart, each under a small faint cross (tickets 0719, 0818), so a White bead (drawn as a bead, with its letter) never reads as empty and an empty position never as a White bead.
_Avoid_: Cover page (the PNG before ticket 0915), story, header block (the old PNG's name for the text under the chart)

**Clean up stray beads** (ticket 0906, spec 0761, ADR 0010 D-2): the converter's helper that gives a bead standing alone (no bead of its own color against any side, through its Technique's neighbours) the color most of its colored neighbours have, a tie going to the color nearest its own, so a converted photo has no bead that is a thread change by itself. Two beads in a line and a one-bead-wide outline stay; empty places (a removed background, a color made empty) are never filled and do not vote. Its strength is **Light**, **Medium** or **Strong**, one, two and three passes of the rule. It runs on the beads after the match, so the found colors, their counts, Color by color and Beads needed follow. **Small colors join** sits beside it, in the same **Clean up** popover under Colors: a color used by fewer beads than its number (10, from "off" to 50) joins the nearest color that stays. A photo opens with both on (Medium, 10), flat art (ticket 0889's detector) with both off, so pixel art keeps every pixel; what the person sets is kept for that picture and a new picture opens at its own defaults. Neither is a step of the Background popover's Undo and Redo.
_Avoid_: denoise, smoothing, despeckle

**Auto-fix lighting** (ticket 0907, spec 0761, ADR 0010 D-2): the converter's one switch that corrects the picture before it becomes beads, for a photo taken under a warm or dim light: it balances white from the picture's own pixels, lifts a dark middle toward a set brightness (never darkening) with the black kept black, fades the color of the deep shadows (the camera's noise) and stretches chroma so the most saturated few percent reach a set target. It sits in the column's picture section, under the picture row. Off for every picture until the person turns it on; it never turns itself on and is not remembered, so flat art's colors are never changed behind anyone's back. While on, the beads are sampled from the lit picture, so the background found, Clean up, the found colors and their counts follow. Behind it, in the Lighting popover of its sun button, are Brightness, Contrast and Saturation (ticket 0908): sliders from −50 to +50 set by hand after it (with the switch off too), each resting at 0, where it changes nothing, with its own reset; they belong to the picture and are not remembered.
_Avoid_: auto-enhance, filter, white balance
